# Facilitator Guide

Running an AI enablement session is less like teaching software and more like change management with a keyboard. The content is the easy part — the room is the hard part. This guide is what to do before, during, and after.

## Before the session

- **Know your audience's anxiety, not just their skill level.** Pull the top 2–3 open-text concerns from the needs-assessment survey and address them explicitly, by name, early in the session. Unaddressed fear is the biggest blocker to adoption — more than lack of skill.
- **Bring examples from their world, not generic ones.** A prompt example relevant to their actual daily task lands ten times better than a generic "write me a poem" demo.
- **Test everything live, beforehand.** If you're demoing a workflow, run it yourself right before the session. Tools change; a failed live demo undermines trust fast.
- **Set up for the room you have.** Virtual sessions need more explicit check-ins ("type a thumbs up in chat if that made sense"); in-person sessions need visible screens and printed prompt templates as a fallback.

## During the session

- **Open with a real stake, not a AI-hype tone.** Start from a problem they already have, then show AI solving it — never start with "AI is transforming everything."
- **Let them write the first prompt themselves, badly, before you show the "right" way.** Struggling first makes the framework land harder than watching it done perfectly.
- **Narrate your own thinking out loud when you demo.** "I'm adding this constraint because last time the output was too long" teaches the iteration mindset, not just the tool.
- **Watch for silent disengagement**, not just people saying they're lost. In virtual sessions, low chat activity is often a signal to slow down, not speed up.

## Common questions — and how to answer them

**"Will this replace my job?"**
Answer honestly: AI changes which parts of the job take time, it doesn't remove the judgment, relationships, or accountability that are actually the job. Give a concrete example from their own role of a task AI helps with vs. one it can't touch.

**"Is it safe to use with our data?"**
Point to the actual org policy, in plain language: what's approved, what's never allowed, and who to ask if unsure. Never leave this vague — vagueness is what causes shadow IT usage.

**"What if the AI gets it wrong?"**
Teach the evaluation habit explicitly: AI output is a draft, not a deliverable, until a human has checked it against a simple standard (accuracy, tone, completeness). This is a skill you can practice, not just a warning to remember.

**"I tried it once and it didn't work."**
Ask what prompt they used. Nine times out of ten, the fix is specificity — more context, a clearer format request, or an example of the output they wanted. Reframe "it didn't work" as "the prompt needs one more iteration," which is true almost every time.

## After the session

- Send a one-page recap within 24 hours: the framework, one worked example, and where to get help.
- Log attendance and self-reported confidence (before/after) — this feeds the Adoption Scorecard.
- Follow up individually with anyone who was visibly stuck — a two-minute check-in prevents quiet disengagement from becoming quiet non-adoption.
